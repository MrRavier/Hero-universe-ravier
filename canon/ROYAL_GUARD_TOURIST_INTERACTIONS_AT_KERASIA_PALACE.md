# Royal Guard and tourist interactions at the Royal Palace of Kerasia

## Continuity
The Royal Palace of Kerasia is a Crown property and one of the most photographed sites in Sintica, especially after 2022. The public square and outer perimeter are normally accessible, while the family wing, working wing, security zones and subterranean areas remain closed. The Royal Guard is both ceremonial and genuinely protective; its visual language follows Sintica's neo-historical state aesthetic while using modern communications, protection and security procedures.

## Tourist behavior
A recurring feature of palace tourism is visitors trying to provoke visible reactions from sentries, similar in spirit to the familiar tourist behavior around famous ceremonial guards elsewhere in Europe.

Common harmless behavior includes:
- exaggerated waving;
- funny faces;
- posing for photographs very close to the marked public limit;
- speaking loudly to a guard in English, French, German, Serbian, Macedonian, Portuguese or other languages;
- asking whether the guard is real;
- trying to make the guard smile;
- children saluting them;
- tourists making short videos or social-media challenges such as "make a Sintican Royal Guard laugh".

The Guard generally ignores this so long as the visitor remains outside restricted space and does not interfere with duty.

## Sentry discipline
Public-facing sentries are trained to remain still and professional during harmless attempts at provocation. They are not expected to behave like statues at the cost of security; observation, radio use, movement and verbal commands take priority whenever necessary.

A sentry may visibly react when:
- a person crosses the marked perimeter;
- someone attempts to touch the guard, weapon, uniform or equipment;
- a visitor blocks a patrol route or ceremonial movement;
- someone approaches a gate or checkpoint in a manner inconsistent with public access;
- there is a credible security concern or suspicious use of an extraordinary ability;
- a child, elderly person or vulnerable visitor needs immediate help.

The standard reaction to minor boundary violations is a sharp verbal command and repositioning, not gratuitous force.

## The line tourists eventually discover
A typical viral scene in Kerasia has a tourist spending several minutes trying unsuccessfully to make a sentry laugh. The guard remains motionless while the tourist waves, tells jokes and poses beside the marked line.

Then the tourist puts one shoe across the boundary.

The sentry instantly turns his head and says, in a much louder voice than expected:

"ATRÁS DA LINHA."

The tourist jumps back. The guard immediately resumes position.

These clips become common on travel accounts because they show the contrast between the picturesque historical uniform and the fact that the soldier is actually on duty.

## Touching the guard
Tourists are repeatedly warned not to touch sentries. Harmless proximity for photographs is tolerated only where the layout permits it, but grabbing an arm, tapping a helmet, touching a weapon, hanging on a shoulder or attempting to remove an item triggers immediate intervention.

This can involve:
- a direct command;
- physically creating distance;
- another guard or nearby police officer stepping in;
- removal from the immediate palace perimeter;
- identification or citation if conduct becomes disorderly.

Sintican authorities do not treat ordinary foolish tourism as a major crime, but they do not allow the Royal Guard to become a prop people can handle.

## Children
Children are an exception in tone, not in security rules.

A child who salutes, asks a question or drops a toy near a guard may receive a brief nod or quiet assistance when duty permits. These moments are especially popular with tourists because the same guard who ignored ten adults trying to make him laugh may bend down to return a dropped toy to a four-year-old.

This does not undermine discipline; it is treated as ordinary judgment.

## Changing of the guard
The Palace maintains a formal changing-of-the-guard ceremony on selected days and occasions. It is a genuine transfer of sentry responsibility, but also a tourism draw.

The ceremony uses:
- historically styled parade uniforms;
- precise marching and posted arms;
- a small military band on major occasions;
- formal relief of sentries;
- modern security personnel remaining less visibly positioned around the perimeter.

After 2022, attendance increases enough that municipal barriers, marked viewing lanes and additional police presence are sometimes used during peak tourism periods.

## Other official buildings
Similar scenes occur, though usually on a smaller scale, at other buildings where the Royal Guard or ceremonial state personnel are posted for official functions.

Possible locations include:
- entrances used by the Crown during major government sessions;
- memorial ceremonies;
- state funerals;
- high-level diplomatic visits;
- selected historic state buildings during national holidays.

The Palace remains the best-known location because permanent public-facing sentry duty and tourism overlap there.

## Social-media culture
Sintican social media develops recurring categories of clips:
- "Guard did not move for five minutes";
- "Tourist crossed the line and instantly regretted it";
- "Kid salutes Royal Guard";
- "Trying five languages on the same sentry";
- "Royal Guard almost smiled";
- tourists debating whether a tiny change in mouth or eyebrow counted as a reaction.

The Royal Household does not encourage harassment of guards, but it accepts that the public-facing sentry tradition is part of the Palace's modern tourism identity.

## Mihail's attitude
Mihail does not object to tourists taking photographs, joking from the legal public area or trying harmlessly to get a reaction. He considers that preferable to turning the palace frontage into an inaccessible security zone.

He is less amused when visitors touch personnel or deliberately test security boundaries.

A recurring private joke among palace staff is that tourists spend hours attempting to make the Royal Guard react while Mihail, who lives inside, can make half the detail move instantly simply by appearing where nobody expected him.

## Canon guardrails
- The Royal Guard is not purely ceremonial; sentries are genuine security personnel.
- Harmless tourist antics are tolerated within marked public space.
- Touching a guard, equipment or weapon is not tolerated.
- Security reactions are professional and proportionate, not theatrical brutality.
- Modern security operates behind the historical visual presentation.
- The Royal Palace remains a functioning residence and state site, not a theme park.
