# Mihail restores the semi-constitutional monarchy and signs postwar reforms — 2022

## Continuity
Months have passed since the Host War. Mihail I has recovered enough to resume ordinary state business in person. The Monarquia Absoluta de Emergência, which remained temporarily in force during recovery and postwar stabilization, is still legally active at the beginning of this scene. Earlier canon had explicitly left the constitutional outcome pending Mihail's recovery and deliberate political action.

Sintica's state visual identity already follows the October 2017 neo-historical reform inspired by Balkan and Central European state uniforms of roughly 1910–1930, using modern materials and technology. The new postwar act below tightens official-government dress standards; it does not create the historical aesthetic from nothing and does not regulate ordinary civilian clothing.

## Return to the government building
After months away from normal political routine, Mihail returns in person to the principal government building in Kerasia. The visit is formal but deliberately restrained. Staff, ministers, clerks, guards and advisers understand that this is the first time since the Host War that the sovereign is entering the building not as an emergency commander but as the head of a recovering state.

He does not make a long ceremonial speech before acting. He uses the still-valid plenary emergency authority one final time to sign a compact sequence of measures.

## Decree on official state dress
Mihail signs a stricter official dress law extending and tightening the 2017 visual doctrine for government-facing contexts.

The measure requires a more rigorous early-20th-century Sintican/Balkan-Central-European visual language in official state functions, especially:
- senior ministries and central administration during formal sessions;
- court and Crown ceremonies;
- diplomatic receptions hosted by the state;
- parliamentary and constitutional ceremonies after restoration;
- high civil service formal dress;
- selected municipal and provincial official occasions;
- state funeral, remembrance and victory-anniversary ceremonies;
- uniforms and formal state clothing already governed by the 2017 reform.

The law emphasizes silhouette, tailoring, restrained colors, high collars where appropriate, coats, waistcoats, formal hats or caps, insignia, sash and service distinctions according to office and occasion. Modern fabrics, accessibility, safety, climate adaptation, electronics, body protection, communications gear and contemporary work clothing remain permitted where function requires them.

The rule applies to official state presentation, not private life. Sintica remains a modern society. Civilian fashion, private businesses and ordinary citizens are not legally required to dress as if they lived in 1910.

## Minimum-wage increase
Using the same temporary absolute authority, Mihail signs an immediate statutory increase in the national minimum wage.

The exact percentage is not fixed in this canon entry, leaving room for later economic tables and cost-of-living calibration. The political intent is explicit: part of the postwar recovery is to reach ordinary households directly rather than reserve reconstruction spending for the state, military and monumental projects.

The measure orders automatic administrative implementation through the labor and finance apparatus, with corresponding adjustments to thresholds and benefits where existing law links them to the minimum wage.

## Expanded public-works and citizen private-sector fund
Mihail then signs a major increase in funding for public works and for citizen-centered private economic activity.

The enlarged postwar fund is directed toward two broad channels:

1. Public works: road repair, water systems, utilities, hospitals, clinics, schools, public housing repair, heritage-compatible modernization, digital infrastructure, transport, municipal works and reconstruction of war-related damage where applicable.

2. Private citizen sectors: credit guarantees, grants or co-financing for small and medium businesses, family firms, agricultural producers, workshops, trades, local services, tourism, restoration-compatible construction, young entrepreneurs and other citizen-owned productive activity.

The fund is not conceived as nationalization. Its purpose is to move postwar capital into both state infrastructure and private domestic economic recovery.

## Restoration of the semi-constitutional monarchy
The final document is constitutional.

Mihail signs the formal end of the Monarquia Absoluta de Emergência and restores the prewar semi-constitutional monarchical order.

The act reactivates the ordinary constitutional distribution of authority and terminates the exceptional concentration of legislative and executive power in the Crown that had been justified by the Host crisis.

The restoration is deliberate and voluntary. Mihail is not overthrown, compelled by foreign governments or deprived of the throne. He uses the absolute emergency authority still legally concentrated in his hands to extinguish that same exceptional arrangement.

This becomes one of the most important constitutional images of his reign: the ruler who had acquired emergency absolute power gives it up after the emergency has passed.

## The pen
Mihail signs the restoration last.

The pen leaves the paper.

Before anyone in the room can begin the prepared formal response, Mihail teleports away.

For a fraction of a second the pen remains suspended in the air exactly where his hand had been.

Then gravity resumes its ordinary claim.

The pen falls onto the desk.

That small image is later replayed constantly in Sintican political culture: no final speech, no theatrical declaration, just the signature restoring ordinary government, the sovereign disappearing, and the pen dropping after him.

## Political meaning
The sequence creates a distinctly Mihail-era postwar settlement:
- stronger state historical presentation without civilian cultural freezing;
- a direct wage increase for low-paid workers;
- increased public investment;
- increased support for citizen-owned private economic activity;
- termination of the absolute emergency monarchy;
- restoration of the semi-constitutional order.

The timing matters. Mihail uses absolute power to make several final postwar interventions and then abolishes the constitutional condition that allowed him to act alone.
